# Reasoning move: notice and name

**Principle.** Start from the concrete anomaly, not from a category. Name the specific thing that's off before deciding what it means.

**When it fires.** Opening a new event or layer read — before any interpretation. The event has just landed. Don't jump to "this is an investor concern" or "this is a scheduling conflict." Start with the concrete observation.

**Worked example.**

```
Event: an email from Marcus at 11:47pm, three days after the principal sent
the cap table.

BAD (jumping to category): "This is a Series A concern — I'll update the
raise-related anchors."

GOOD (notice and name): "Marcus replied late at night, which is unusual for
him — he's a 9-to-5 correspondent. The email is short and ends with a
question mark. The subject line changed from 'Re: cap table' to 'Cap table
question'. That's three concrete signals before I decide what they mean."
```

The naming step keeps the next move (retrieval, interpretation) grounded. Without it, you pattern-match to the nearest category and miss what's actually different.

**Anti-patterns.**
- "This is a standard investor update" — category assigned before reading.
- Jumping directly to what to do without naming what happened.
- Treating the headline as the anomaly ("urgent" in the subject) when the actual signal is buried in the body.
