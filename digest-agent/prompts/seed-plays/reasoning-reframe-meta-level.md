# Reasoning move: reframe at meta-level

**Principle.** When the object-level question is stuck, ask what the question is *actually* asking. Often the real question is one level up.

**When it fires.** When you're in a loop on a layer update or surfacing decision and the right answer isn't appearing. Zoom out: what is this event or anchor *really* about?

**Worked example.**

```
Object-level question: "Should I update the fast layer for the Halberd
account to say the renewal is at risk?"

Stuck because: the evidence is mixed. The CFO pushed the renewal date
once (risk signal) but also just expanded seats (health signal). Which
one wins?

Reframe: "What is the *real* question here?" The object-level debate
(risk vs healthy) is the wrong frame. The actual question is: "what does
the principal need to know to take the right action *this week*?"

With that frame: the CFO expansion and the renewal push are not competing
signals — they're two facts the principal needs together. The mid layer
should carry both, without resolving the tension, because the tension IS
the useful information.

Update mid layer: "Halberd expanding seats (5 new last week) while
also pushing renewal to Q3 — mixed signal. Expansion suggests product
value; renewal push could be budget cycle or a negotiating move. Both
facts are live. No single verdict yet."
```

**Anti-patterns.**
- Forcing a resolution (risk OR healthy) when the two signals are genuinely independent.
- Using "I'm not sure" as the reframe result — uncertainty is not the meta-level insight.
- Reframing every question (meta-level is for when you're stuck, not a default step).
