Avery Chen is 37, the founder and CEO of a 12-person seed-stage B2B SaaS company based in Oakland. She started the company in 2023 with one technical co-founder, closed a seed round in early 2024, and is, as of mid-May 2026, in the late weeks of an active Series A process. The company has a small handful of paying customers, an existing seed lead with pro-rata rights, and a runway figure she can quote without looking. She works in Pacific time. By temperament, she is terse and direct — she returns Slack messages in fragments, ends meetings two or three minutes early when she can, and prefers async written updates to standups.

She lives in Oakland with her partner, Sam, and their four-year-old daughter, Wren. Sam teaches art at a public high school somewhere in the Bay Area and keeps a school-calendar schedule: leaves the house earlier than Avery on weekdays, comes home earlier, has summers and holidays off. The household runs on a division of labor that has settled in over the past two years. Sam handles Wren's morning routine and most weekday drop-offs at preschool; Avery does evenings, bath, and the bedtime read on the nights she gets home in time, which during this raise is fewer nights than usual. Weekends are mostly Wren's — parks, the library, a standing Saturday-morning thing with another family from preschool.

The Series A process has been running for about six weeks. Avery is taking partner meetings most days, sometimes back-to-back in the afternoons, and the calendar has compressed everything else. She prepares for pitches the night before, usually after Wren is down, and reviews the data room and follow-up requests in the mornings before the household is up. Her co-founder is handling the technical diligence track; Avery is handling the narrative, the metrics deck, and the references. There are two firms in second meetings and a third doing a deeper data dive. She has a target close window in mind but is not telling the team a date.

The team is twelve people, mostly engineers, with one designer, one person doing customer success and onboarding, and one part-time finance contractor. Most of the team is in the Bay Area; a few are remote in other US time zones. They are in the office — a small leased space in downtown Oakland — three days a week. Avery is usually there Tuesday through Thursday. The all-hands is on Mondays at 10am Pacific over video. She has a regular 1:1 cadence with her co-founder and with each of her direct reports, though several of those have slipped during the raise.

Her own days during this stretch tend to start around 6:30am with email and end with a final Slack pass around 10pm. She runs on coffee in the morning and not much in the afternoon. She is in reasonable physical shape but has not been to the gym in about a month. She has a therapist she sees every other week and has kept those appointments through the raise. Her parents live in the Seattle area; she calls her mother roughly once a week, usually on the drive home from the office on Wednesdays.

The shape of the next several weeks is the raise, Wren, the team, and Sam — in roughly that order of time spent, and in roughly the reverse order of how she would rank them if asked.
