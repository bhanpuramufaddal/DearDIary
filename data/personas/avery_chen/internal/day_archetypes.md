# Day archetypes — Avery Chen

## Office day in downtown Oakland

Tuesdays and Thursdays usually look like this. Avery is up at 6:30, comes down to the kitchen on Lawton Avenue while Sam is still moving Wren toward the front door, and makes a pour-over from the Highwire beans they keep in a tin next to the grinder. She stands at the counter with the coffee and reads Slack on her laptop — never her phone if she can help it — while Sam negotiates the shoes question with Wren and gets her into the Outback for the 8:30 drop at Rockridge Little School on College. By the time the house is empty Avery is usually thirty minutes into the morning triage, and the next forty minutes are deck work or a draft memo at the kitchen table. She is out the door by 8:15. On Tuesdays she takes the Volvo because she will stop at Market Hall on College on the way home; on Thursdays she walks to Rockridge BART and takes the Orange line one stop to 19th Street, six blocks from the office on 17th Street near Frank Ogawa Plaza.

She is at her desk by 8:55 and works heads-down from 9:00 to about 10:30 — the second of her two real thinking blocks, the one she protects on the calendar. Priya is usually in by 9:30, and the two of them trade Slack fragments for an hour without standing up. Avery goes downstairs to Caffe Teatro in the Rotunda for the second coffee around 11:00, takes it black, and does not have another after that. Lunch is a Mixt salad from the Broadway location, ordered ahead and eaten at the desk while reading; the afternoon between 2:00 and 5:30 is partner meetings, diligence calls, and 1:1s with Devon or Anika or Jenna in the small glass room at the back of the suite. She does not schedule new work after 4:00. On Tuesdays she leaves at 5:35, parks on College outside Market Hall, picks up something for Wren's lunchbox and whatever Sam has texted her to grab, and is in the kitchen by 6:15. On Thursdays she is on a 19th Street train by 5:45 and home by 6:10, in time for the second half of Wren's dinner and the bath.

## Monday at home

She works from Lawton Avenue on Mondays. The morning runs the same as any weekday — up at 6:30, Highwire pour-over, Sam and Wren out the door at 8:15 — except she does not get up from the kitchen table at the end of it. Her desk is in the small office off the kitchen, and she has the door closed by 8:45. She uses the stretch from 8:45 to 9:55 to clear the weekend's Slack backlog and to write the priorities note she keeps in Notion for herself, which is the one document she has been disciplined about for almost two years. The Holdfast all-hands is at 10:00 a.m. Pacific over Zoom, runs forty-five minutes most weeks, and Avery talks for about twelve of them — a single update on the company's quarter, a hand-off to Priya for engineering, and questions at the end. She closes the laptop at 10:50 and is back at the desk by 11:00.

The middle of the day is meetings she actually wanted on the calendar — a hiring conversation, a longer 1:1 she could not fit in the office, sometimes a customer reference call she takes from the quiet of the house. Lunch is leftovers from Sunday standing at the counter, usually in under ten minutes, while she scrolls X. Her 1:1 with Priya is on the calendar Mondays at 4:00 p.m. and runs the full hour when neither of them has reschuffled it; they take it on Zoom even though Priya is in Berkeley and could drive over, because the shared screen is how they actually work. Sam is home with Wren by 4:45, and Avery comes out of the office around 5:30 to take the bath-and-bedtime shift she almost always gets on Mondays. She is usually back at the laptop from 9:00 to 10:30 for the final Slack pass and the next morning's priorities list.

## Wednesday drive day with the call to Linda

Wednesdays she drives the Volvo to the office. The morning is the same as any office day — up at 6:30, the Highwire pour-over, the kitchen-counter triage while Sam handles Wren — but she leaves at 8:00 rather than 8:15 because the 580-to-980 stretch into downtown thickens by 8:30. She parks in the garage on 17th and is upstairs by 8:35. The thinking block is the same 9:00-to-10:30 window; the second coffee from Caffe Teatro comes around 11:00; lunch is Mixt at the desk. The afternoon is the same partner-meeting and 1:1 stack as the other office days, and she has been good about keeping a clean window from 5:00 to 5:30 to wrap the day rather than starting something new.

She is in the car by 5:35 and calls her mother on the drive home, usually somewhere on the 980 ramp. The call runs about twenty minutes — Linda asks twice about whether Avery has been sleeping and then changes the subject to Wren, and Avery is patient about two times out of three. David sometimes takes the phone for the last two or three minutes; the conversation with him is logistical and ends quickly. Avery is in the driveway on Lawton by 6:05, off the call by the time she opens the front door, and into the bath-and-bedtime shift on the nights she has made it back in time. She does not listen to anything on the drive in or out on Wednesdays; the Acquired episodes she is behind on accumulate for the other solo drives.

## Therapy Thursday

Every other Thursday is the therapy-Thursday variant of the office day. The morning and the workday run identically — Highwire at the counter, BART to 19th Street, the 9:00-to-10:30 thinking block, the Caffe Teatro coffee, the Mixt salad, the partner meetings stacked through the afternoon — except Avery puts a hard stop on the calendar at 4:50. She walks the eight blocks from 17th Street to the BART rather than waiting on a train at rush, gets off at MacArthur instead of Rockridge, and is at Dr. Reyes's office on Telegraph in Temescal at 5:25. The appointment is at 5:30 and runs fifty minutes; sometimes it is in person and sometimes it is on Zoom from the office if the schedule has compressed in a way that makes the trip across town hard. The cadence has held for almost three years; she has not rescheduled an appointment in 2026.

She walks the five blocks down Telegraph to Forty-Sixth Street afterward, calls a Lyft, and is on Lawton by 6:50. Sam has done Wren's dinner and is most of the way through bath; Avery takes over for the bedtime read on the nights she gets back in time, which is most therapy-Thursdays. She eats whatever Sam set aside for her at the counter after Wren is down, and is back at the laptop by 8:45 for the evening Slack pass. The end-of-day on a therapy Thursday is shorter than the other weeknights — usually four or five messages and the priorities note for Friday — because the appointment has taken the same forty minutes she would otherwise have spent on a partner-prep email.

## Friday wind-down

Fridays are shorter on both ends. Avery is up at 6:30 as usual but does not push as hard on the kitchen-table block; she lets Sam's Wren routine spill into hers, and the two of them sometimes get fifteen minutes at the counter before Sam leaves for Berkeley High. She is on BART to 19th Street by 8:30 — Friday is a BART day by habit — and into the office by 9:00. The morning is the week's last cleanup work, the diligence requests that came in Thursday afternoon and that Priya wants her eyes on, and the Slack messages from Maya Okafor that have been waiting two or three days. The all-hands recap goes out in a brief Notion post she writes most Fridays before lunch. She listens to Hard Fork on the BART ride in.

She leaves the office by 4:30 most Fridays and is home on Lawton by 5:00, in time to take Wren on the short walk to the Khans' four blocks away on Manila if Maya Khan's parents Rohan and Lila are running late, or to put on the kettle if it is a Sam-makes-dinner night. Friday dinner is takeout — Zachary's Pizza on College most weeks, picked up by Sam on his way home, eaten at the kitchen table with Wren — and has held through the last six weeks without slipping once. Avery pours sparkling water in a wine glass and has not said anything about it since January. They are at bath by 6:30 and Wren is down by 7:00. The half-hour after dinner used to be the open part of the evening; it is now about twenty minutes most Fridays and Avery is usually back at her laptop by 8:30 to clear the week and write Monday's priorities note.

## Saturday morning at Little Frog Park

Saturdays are Wren's. Avery is up at 7:00 — half an hour later than weekdays, almost never longer — and makes the Highwire pour-over for both of them while Sam puts Wren's hair up and finds her shoes. Breakfast is the same Greek yogurt with frozen blueberries and almond butter Avery eats at the counter every other day of the week, eaten sitting down on Saturday because nothing is moving fast. The three of them are out the door by 9:30 and walk the ten minutes to Little Frog Park on Claremont Avenue, where the standing thing with Rohan and Lila Khan and their daughter Maya has held since the spring of 2024. The Khans bring the snack most weeks. Avery and Lila stand near the bench by the toddler structure and trade short observations about the kids and longer ones about the week; Sam and Rohan tend to walk the perimeter. The four of them are usually back on their separate blocks by 11:30.

When Avery is running, she runs the Lake Merritt loop on Saturday mornings — three point one miles, flat, starting from the Sailboat House at Lakeside Park — and either does it before the park, leaving the house at 7:15 and back by 8:30, or skips a Saturday in favor of doing the run Sunday afternoon. She runs without headphones. The rest of Saturday is Wren-led — a trip to the children's section at the Rockridge branch of the Oakland Public Library if it is raining, a longer stop at the playground if it is not, sometimes a Saturday-afternoon handoff to Sam's mother Diane when she drives down from Walnut Creek. Avery usually opens her laptop once between 3:00 and 5:00 to clear Slack and to read whatever Bessemer or the other partner tracks have sent over the morning, and closes it before dinner.

## Sunday at the library and the Sunday-night reset

Sundays are the slowest day of Avery's week and the one she has been most disciplined about keeping for the household. The morning runs without an alarm; she and Sam are usually both up by 7:30, and the three of them eat at the kitchen table — pancakes Sam makes once or twice a month, eggs and toast otherwise. Avery does the Lake Merritt loop on Sunday afternoons when she did not run Saturday; she goes around 2:00, leaves Wren with Sam, and is back by 3:30. On the Sundays when nothing is scheduled she walks Wren to the Rockridge branch of the Oakland Public Library on College Avenue around 3:30, lets Wren pick out four picture books — the dinosaur ones most weeks — and reads The Atlantic on her phone in the kid-section bean bag while Wren turns pages on the carpet next to her. They are home by 4:45.

Sam does Sunday dinner — usually a roast chicken with sheet-pan vegetables, sometimes the turkey chili Wren tolerates but does not love — and the three of them eat early, around 5:30, so Wren can be in bath by 6:30 and down by 7:00. Trash and recycling go out before dinner, Sunday night for Monday morning pickup. Avery is at her laptop in the small office off the kitchen from about 8:00 to 10:00 most Sunday evenings, writing the week's priorities note in Notion, sketching the Monday all-hands update, and reading whatever has arrived in the data room from the diligence team. She tries to be in bed by 10:30 on Sundays and is, by her Oura ring, on her best sleep night of the week roughly two Sundays out of three.
