## Inner circle (P0)

### Sam Whitlock

Sam is Avery's husband, forty-one, the art teacher at Berkeley High School and the visual arts department lead there since fall 2024. They met in October 2014 in Brooklyn at a friend-of-a-friend's housewarming in Fort Greene; he was teaching at a charter middle school in Bed-Stuy. They moved in together in July 2016, moved to the Bay Area together in 2018, married at his aunt's house in Sebastopol in 2019. Wren was born August 2021. They live on Lawton Avenue in Rockridge; he handles Wren's morning routine and drops her at Rockridge Little School on College Avenue most weekdays before biking up to Berkeley High.

The household runs on a division of labor that has held since around the time Avery left Plaid in late 2023 to start Holdfast. Sam does breakfast and drop-off; Avery does bath and bedtime on the nights she gets home. During the raise, those nights are mostly Wednesday and Friday, sometimes Tuesday. Sam handles dinner roughly four nights a week. Friday-night dinner — usually Zachary's Pizza on College, sometimes a roast chicken Sam puts together — has held through the six weeks of the raise. They have not had a date night without Wren since late February.

The texture of the marriage right now is steady but compressed. They have not had a fight in close to a year. The half-hour of unstructured talking after Wren is down used to be the open part of the evening; it is now about ten minutes most nights and about twenty on Fridays. Sam raised this once, on a Sunday morning in early April in the kitchen before Wren was up, and said the version of it he says — direct, calm, not phrased as a complaint. Avery has not raised it back. She mentioned the rescheduling of the larger conversation to Dr. Reyes by name at her May 14 appointment.

Sam is, on Avery's standing account, steady and generous, a good father, an artist by training who chose teaching. All of that is true. He is also — by his sister Hannah's account more than by Avery's — a more visibly anxious person on long stretches than Avery treats him as. He has not painted seriously, meaning more than an hour at a time in the garage studio, in close to eighteen months. Avery suggested in spring 2024 that he take a semester off to paint; Sam said in the kitchen that the suggestion sounded like it had been made without a full accounting of the household's cash flow. Neither has raised the painting question since.

He noticed Avery stopped drinking about two weeks in and asked once. She said she felt better. He has not asked again. He texts her observations about Wren in the middle of the workday — usually one line, sometimes a photo — and she replies inside two minutes by her own observed pattern, including during partner meetings. The thing about Sam Avery does not say out loud, and that Sam does not say at all, is that Sam's willingness to absorb the schedule is doing more of the household's structural work than Avery gives him credit for.
