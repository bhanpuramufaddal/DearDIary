# How Avery Chen decides what matters this month

Avery reads email twice a day during this stretch, once at the kitchen counter around 6:30 after Sam has left with Wren and once around 4:00 from the desk in the Oakland office or, on the days she is home, from the kitchen counter again. She does not read it on her phone if she can help it. The phone gets read in between, which is the part of her own behavior she has been watching most closely the last ten days. What is actually controlling her attention this month is the gap between the Bessemer second partner meeting on May 19, which went well enough that she is now waiting on a term sheet, and the Greylock second meeting on May 21, which went well enough that Greylock is likely to make an offer if Bessemer does not. Almost every other allocation question in her week traces back to that wait.

The two things that, if she misses them, the month goes badly are the term-sheet response from Bessemer and the Greylock follow-up. The data room responses she owes Bessemer's diligence team by May 22 are the most concrete near-term work item; she has been drafting those in the morning blocks before Wren is up and reviewing them with Priya over Slack in fragments through the day. The Accel second meeting happened the previous week, and Avery is treating Accel as a likely no without saying so to Priya in those words. Cowboy is exercising pro-rata and does not require management. The shape of her week, therefore, is two real partner conversations to land, one diligence deadline, and roughly nine working days of waiting on a piece of paper. Waiting is the part of the process she handles worst, and she knows it well enough to have named it to Dr. Reyes at the appointment on May 14. She has not adjusted her behavior since naming it.

Three people can reach her and get a same-hour response. Priya, her co-founder and CTO, gets a reply inside twenty minutes by default and inside five if the Slack is one line. Priya is running the technical diligence track in parallel, and Avery's reply is on the critical path of about half of Priya's messages. Priya is also the only person who knows the actual target range on the round — $14M at $60M post, with a floor at $12M at $52M — and Avery answers Priya's pricing-related Slacks faster than any other category of message. Sam gets a reply inside two minutes, by Avery's own observed pattern, and that pattern has not changed during the raise; if Sam texts during a partner meeting, Avery still glances. Anything from Rockridge Little School — Ms. Annette and Ms. Joelle on the preschool side, or the school's general account — Avery opens within the hour. Wren had the ear-infection stretch in November and December, and the pattern of opening school email immediately has carried over. Within the school category, Sam is the one who handles pickup most days, and Avery's same-hour reply is usually a one-line confirmation to Sam rather than an action of her own.

The next tier is people she likes and respects who will not hear back for two or three days. Maya Okafor, the growth hire who started January 6, is in this tier this month. Maya is good, and Maya's Slack messages still take Avery between two and three days to answer. Maya over-explains in writing, and her messages run long. Maya has noticed the delay and has begun front-loading the ask in the first sentence, which has shortened Avery's reply latency by about a day on the messages where it has worked. Jordan Reyes, the customer success lead, is in the same tier for a different reason. Avery has rescheduled the 1:1 with Jordan twice in the last six weeks, and in the last three weeks Jordan has begun asking Priya in the office for things he would previously have asked Avery for. Avery has not raised the pattern with Jordan and has not put a new 1:1 on the calendar; the slot that was rescheduled twice is currently unscheduled. Megan Park, the friend Avery has dinner with every six weeks on Piedmont Avenue, falls into a similar bucket — Avery owes Megan a reply about a dinner Megan suggested for the first week of June, and the reply has been sitting in the drafts folder for four days.

What she reliably misses or notices late is also worth being honest about. She subscribes to Stratechery and renews it without thinking, and she has opened it twice in the past month. She subscribes to The Information and skims headlines without reading articles in full. She has the New York Times morning briefing on her phone and reads it during the BART ride on the days she takes BART, which during this stretch is Wednesdays and Thursdays. The Acquired podcast accumulates in her queue and gets played in the car on Wednesday drives home; she is currently about two episodes behind. She has not opened her Bluesky account in months. She updates LinkedIn twice a year and has not updated it this month. She has a Peloton in the garage that she has not used in about six weeks and a climbing-gym membership that auto-renews at $109 a month and that she has not used since late March. She has been twenty pages into Demon Copperhead on the bedside table since November. None of this is unusual for the stretch she is in; what is worth noting is which of it she will not catch up on once the round closes and which of it she will. The newsletters she will not catch up on. The Peloton she will, probably in the second week after the term sheet is signed. The book she will finish in Sea Ranch from June 12 to June 15, if the round is signed by then.

The shape of the day matters more than the shape of the week. She has two real thinking blocks. One is the stretch between 6:30, when she comes down to the kitchen, and 8:15, when she leaves the house — a roughly hour-and-three-quarters block, of which the first thirty minutes are email triage, the next forty are usually deck or memo work, and the last twenty are getting out the door. The other is the stretch between 8:30 and 9:30 in the office on the days she is in, which is Tuesday through Thursday. The afternoon hours from 2:00 to 5:30 are mostly partner meetings or diligence calls during this stretch and are not thinking hours. She does not schedule new work after 4:00. She will answer Slack after 4:00, but she will not draft a partner email or a board memo. She drinks coffee twice in a day, once at the house and once at the office, both before noon, and the second cup is the cutoff. She has not had a drink since January 4 and pours sparkling water in a wine glass at Friday-night dinner without commenting on it. Sam asked once about it in mid-January and has not asked again.

Wednesdays she drives to the office rather than taking BART because she calls her mother on the way home. The call is roughly twenty minutes, and Linda asks twice about whether Avery has been sleeping and then changes the subject to Wren. Avery is patient on the call about two times out of three. The Wednesday-evening commute is therefore not a thinking block; the call has its own cadence and uses the time. Tuesdays she also drives, because she usually stops at Market Hall on College on the way home, and Thursdays she takes BART. The therapy appointment with Dr. Reyes every other Thursday at 5:30 has held through the raise; Avery has not rescheduled it once. The 1:1 with Priya, which is supposed to be weekly and has been weekly more often than not, is on the calendar for Monday May 18 at 4:00 and has not been rescheduled in two cycles. The all-hands at 10:00 a.m. on Monday over Zoom has not slipped at all.

The frictions taking more attention than they should this month are specific and few. The kitchen tile question — pale green hexagons the previous owners put in, that she and Sam have discussed replacing twice and visited two showrooms about — has not advanced in over a year and is not on the list. The contractor question is therefore not active. The friction with Jordan Reyes is the largest of the work ones, because the redirect to Priya is a real signal that Avery has noticed and that she has not yet acted on, and because the customer success function is the one Holdfast will need to scale most visibly after the round closes. Avery knows this. She has not put time on it this month and is not going to. The friction with Maya is smaller and Avery is closer to acting on it; the front-loaded-ask pattern has helped. The friction with Sam is the second-largest in absolute terms and the one Avery talks about least. Sam raised the shrinking unstructured time in the kitchen one Sunday morning in early April, before Wren was up, and has not raised it since. Avery has not raised it back. She mentioned the rescheduling of the larger conversation to Dr. Reyes on May 14 by name. The conversation has not been put on the calendar.

The standing biases in her judgment matter for which of the above she handles well. She overweights writing over speech. The narrative document Holdfast used to close the two enterprise contracts in October came out of three weekends of revising on the kitchen counter, and the seed investors quote sentences out of it back to her. Inside the company, the consequence of this bias is that engineers who write well get more of her attention per unit of work than engineers who do not, and a junior engineer named Caleb has received two more pieces of direct feedback from her in the last quarter than the rest of the engineering team combined, in part because his code-review comments are well-constructed. She has not adjusted for this. She underweights async messages relative to in-person ones consistently; Priya raised the pattern in a candid Saturday-morning conversation in February 2025, and the pattern has not changed since. This is the mechanism by which Jordan's redirect to Priya in the office has had more weight in Avery's awareness than the same redirect by Slack would have had. She treats praise from senior investors as administrative information and criticism from senior investors as the real signal; Priya has watched her do this with the board. In this month, that means the warm follow-up from the Bessemer partner after May 19 has been read as polite and the one slightly cool sentence from the Greylock side after May 21 has been read repeatedly. She has been opening the phone more often than she did before May 19.

The cadence in the back half of May, then, is roughly this. Mornings until 8:15, drafting and email triage. BART or drive to the office Tuesday through Thursday. Thinking block 8:30 to 9:30. Partner meetings, diligence calls, and 1:1s on the calendar through the afternoon, with the second cup of coffee around 11:00 and a Mixt salad at the desk most days. The Priya 1:1 Monday at 4:00. The Bessemer diligence responses owed Friday. The Greylock second meeting Thursday. The therapy appointment that Thursday at 5:30. Memorial Day weekend is blocked on her calendar Saturday through Monday; she has told Priya she will be off email and intends to be off email and will probably check email twice on Sunday. The conversation with Sam is not on the calendar and is not going to be on the calendar before the term sheet arrives. The TK deposit for Park Day was paid April 14, the Sea Ranch trip is booked for June 12 to 15, and her mother is flying down in the second week of July. The next board meeting is June 24, which she has not started preparing for and which she will start preparing for once she knows what is closing and at what price.
