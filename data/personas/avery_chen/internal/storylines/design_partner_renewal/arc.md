# Design-partner renewal — Tidemark Specialty Insurance ($90K annual)

Tidemark Specialty Insurance is one of Holdfast's pre-October-2025 design-partner customers — a mid-market specialty insurance brokerage headquartered in San Francisco that signed a one-year $90K agreement in May 2025 on a discount-from-list "design-partner" SKU. The contract anniversary is Saturday, May 23, 2026, which is the last day of the window. Under the MSA the renewal must be confirmed at least thirty days before expiry, which is what triggered the renewal-notice email Jordan Reyes sent on Monday April 20. <!-- web-verified 2026-05-25: Mid-market specialty insurance brokerages of Tidemark's size typically run with a VP of Finance + Controller + Procurement structure and execute SaaS renewals via MSA + order-form mechanics (Heffernan Insurance Brokers, EPIC Insurance, Specialty Program Group company profiles; Trintech and Ascend industry write-ups on insurance back-office finance ops). -->

This renewal doubles as a Series A data point. Saam Motamedi at Greylock pressed on the design-partner pricing question in the first meeting in late April — specifically, whether the design-partner accounts will renew at list. <!-- web-verified 2026-05-25: Saam Motamedi is a General Partner at Greylock focused on enterprise SaaS (greylock.com/team/saam-motamedi/). --> Avery wants the renewal closed at list ($90K, no discount, ideally with expanded scope) before the term sheet expected the morning of Friday May 29, so the metrics story stays clean. She has told Priya in one line that this is the most quotable customer signal she can put on the Bessemer track this month. She has not told Jordan that framing in those words.

Stakeholders on Tidemark's side. **Marcus Reilly** is the VP of Finance and the executive sponsor who pushed the original contract through in May 2025; he is direct, brisk, runs his own calendar, and is the one Avery talks to when the conversation needs to move. **Diana Ortiz** is Tidemark's controller and Holdfast's day-to-day user — she runs reconciliations and approvals through the product daily, and her usage pattern is what makes the renewal narrative defensible. **Kevin Liu** is Tidemark's Director of Procurement and Vendor Management, handling the renewal paperwork and the MSA redlines; procurement at Tidemark runs as a function separate from finance, which means Kevin's redlines come in independently of Marcus's pricing position. **Aaron Sandoval** sits on Kevin's team as a contracts/legal-ops associate and is the person who actually drafts the redline language and the data-security addendum. **Lauren Vesper** is Tidemark's CFO; she sits on the MSA signature block but stays out of the back-and-forth.

On Holdfast's side, Jordan Reyes owns the renewal end to end — he is the customer success lead Avery has rescheduled twice in the last six weeks, and this renewal is the one piece of work where she cannot let his function be the bottleneck. Avery is exec sponsor on the closing call and on the moments where Marcus reaches over Jordan to push price. Priya is not in this storyline except as a possible reference if Tidemark asks a feature question Holdfast cannot honestly commit to. Marlene Becker is on the order form and the final invoice. Maya Okafor is downstream — she wants the renewal closed so she can use Tidemark as a quote in the case-study series and on the holdfast.co landing-page refresh.

Phasing. Week 1 (Days 1–5): the 60-day auto-notice goes out from Jordan to Marcus on Monday April 20; Marcus acknowledges April 22 and flags that procurement will be involved this cycle, which was not the case in 2025. Week 2 (Days 6–11): Kevin Liu introduces himself on Monday April 27 with a Tidemark vendor-onboarding packet (a fresh ask, not a 2025 leftover), and the renewal kickoff call is scheduled for Thursday April 30 — then Marcus shifts it one day to Friday May 1. Days 12–18: usage and pricing. Diana sends usage questions May 4; Jordan circulates the renewal proposal at list with an optional new-module add — $90K base, or $99K with the usage-dashboard module shipping later this summer; Marcus pushes back Wednesday May 6 asking for a 10% discount on the design-partner-status grounds. Avery steps in directly Thursday May 7 — she holds list and offers expanded scope at the same price, leaning on the design-partner narrative more squarely than Jordan would. Marcus accepts list on Monday May 11. Days 20–28: procurement redline back-and-forth. Kevin sends MSA redlines Tuesday May 12 with three sticking points — auto-renew on a 60-day notice, mutual liability cap, and a customer-side data-security addendum. The May 14 redline call gets pushed to May 15 by Kevin. Kevin compromises on auto-renew Friday May 15; Aaron Sandoval sends a fresh data-security addendum draft the same afternoon. Avery reviews and sends redlines back Monday May 18. Final terms arrive Wednesday May 20; Marcus signs Thursday May 21. Closing call with Marcus on Friday May 22, fifteen minutes, Avery on as exec sponsor.

Where this could go sideways. Marcus could push harder than expected on price — the 10% ask is the opening move and the design-partner argument cuts both ways. Kevin's redlines on the data-security addendum could surface a SOC 2 gap that bleeds into the parallel SOC 2 Type II audit close-out storyline (the signed audit report does not land until late in the window). Diana's day-to-day usage question on May 19 — Tidemark wants a usage-dashboard preview during the signature push — could pull Priya into a feature commitment Holdfast is not ready to make. And the timing is tight against the Bessemer process: if Tidemark signature slips past Day 32 the renewal stops being available as a closed data point in the investor narrative, and the answer to Saam's question reverts to "expected to renew" rather than "renewed at list."

Digest mornings will cluster around the inflection points. The kickoff-prep morning (Wednesday April 29, then shifted to Thursday April 30 by Marcus's reschedule). The pricing decision (Wednesday May 6, when Marcus's pushback lands, and Thursday May 7, the day Avery owes a reply). The morning Marcus accepts list (Monday May 11 — the most quotable single morning of the storyline, and the line Avery will put in the Friday May 15 investor update). The redline arrival (Tuesday May 12). The addendum review (Monday May 18). And the signature morning (Thursday May 21). The closing-call wrap on Friday May 22 surfaces lightly. Most of the procurement noise — vendor-onboarding form fields, Diana's usage clarifications, Aaron's tracked-changes — will not be digest-worthy on its own.
