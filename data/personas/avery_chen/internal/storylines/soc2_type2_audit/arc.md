The reason this matters is concrete. The Midwest regional bank Holdfast closed in October 2025 — one of the two enterprise contracts that took ARR from $900K to $1.4M — has a vendor-management clause that required a Type II report by June 30, 2026. The bank's information security officer, Daniel Hofstetter, sent a polite calendar-driven follow-up in late March asking when the report would land; Priya replied with a target of "late May, possibly first week of June" and Daniel said that worked. The post-A enterprise pipeline Holdfast wants to start working in July also requires the Type II in hand; two of the three accounts the sales team has been tracking will not take a vendor-security questionnaire seriously without it. Avery is not in the weeds — Priya and Marlene Becker run the engagement day-to-day — but Avery is the one who signs the management representation letter, who gets the final signed report from Tom Halpern at Johanson, and who forwards the PDF to Daniel Hofstetter when it lands.

The phasing across the 35-day window is well-defined. Days 1–14 — Sunday April 19 through Saturday May 2 — are the last evidence-request volleys, with Rachel Singh pinging Priya and Marlene with outstanding-items lists every Tuesday and Friday. Most of these are routine and resolve in 24–48 hours; two land on Avery's desk because they require a CEO signature or a CEO-level acknowledgment (an access-control exception requested by engineering during the observation window; the org-chart and hiring evidence package). Days 18–22 — Wednesday May 6 through Sunday May 10 — are the management-representation-letter exchange: Tom Halpern walks Avery through the rep letter, Avery and Priya review the language over a few days, Avery signs and returns the PDF. The rep letter is the one document in the engagement that has to come from Avery personally and that she cannot delegate. Days 24–28 — Tuesday May 12 through Saturday May 16 — are the draft-report circulation: Tom emails the draft, Priya does a technical pass, Avery skims the executive summary and the management-response section, comments go back. Days 30–32 — Monday May 18 through Wednesday May 20 — are the issuance: signed final SOC 2 Type II report delivered, with a one-day slip risk because Johanson runs internal peer review at the end of every engagement and peer-review notes are not always negotiable. Avery then forwards the PDF to Daniel Hofstetter at the bank within the same day, which closes the customer-side commitment.

What could go sideways is mostly schedule, not substance. The technical evidence is in clean shape — Drata's continuous-monitoring has been live since November, the policy attestation cadence has held through the observation window, and the one production incident inside the window (a four-hour partial outage on February 11 caused by a Postgres failover that didn't fail over cleanly) was classified and remediated correctly. Rachel will ask for the incident write-up and Priya has it ready. The closest thing to a substantive risk is the access-control exception Devon Mehta requested in late February — temporary elevated permissions for a database migration that ran longer than planned — which is documented but which Tom may want Avery to acknowledge in writing as a CEO-level approval. That landed on Avery's desk Friday April 24 and is the first item in the window where Avery has to engage personally. The schedule risk is the peer-review slip at the end: Johanson markets a four-to-six-week turnaround from observation close, which puts the final delivery at the end of May at the latest, and Tom has flagged the third week of May as the target. A one-day slip from Tuesday May 19 to Wednesday May 20 is more likely than not based on how the last two SOC engagements Priya knows about have ended.

The texture is that Avery wants this off her plate. She is in the late weeks of the Series A and the audit is the thing she most wants to be able to forward to a partner firm without a caveat. Priya has been running the engagement competently and Marlene has been running the evidence side competently, and Avery has been treating both of them well by getting out of the way — she signs what they put in front of her inside the same business day, asks clarifying questions only on the items Priya flags as material, and has not been pulling reports out of Drata to second-guess. The Drata account also comes up for renewal in early July at a higher tier (Aaron has been hinting at it since March), and Avery is going to put that conversation off until after the A closes; she has told Priya as much in a one-line Slack on May 1.

The digest mornings this storyline will produce are the days a Johanson email or a Marlene escalation requires Avery's personal attention. That is roughly twelve mornings across the 35-day window, weighted toward the back half, with the if-one-thing landing on Wednesday May 20 — the same week the Bessemer term-sheet wait is at its hottest, which Avery is privately glad about because it means the audit close-out will not have to share oxygen with anything else.
