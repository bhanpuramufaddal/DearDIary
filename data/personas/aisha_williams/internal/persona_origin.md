Aisha Williams is a VP of Product at Google, based in Mountain View. She leads a product area whose annualized revenue surface is in the neighborhood of a billion dollars, which puts her in the tier of VPs who report up through an SVP and who own a portfolio rather than a single product. She has been at the company long enough to have lived through more than one reorg and at least one model-platform shift; she came up through PM ranks, not through a founder-acquisition path. She works in Pacific time. Her calendar is structured around the Google planning cadence — half-year OKRs, quarterly checkpoints, weekly staff, biweekly skip-levels, and the standing executive forums her SVP runs.

She lives in the Bay Area with her partner Marcus and their two children, ages 9 and 12. Marcus is a senior engineering manager at a different tech company, which means the household has two people who understand performance cycles, on-call rotations, and the shape of an exec calendar from the inside. The split of household work is negotiated rather than assumed; both adults default to scheduling things in shared calendars, and both treat school pickup as a real meeting. The 12-year-old is finishing fifth or sixth grade depending on the district and is in the transition window to middle school, which has its own set of orientations, placement decisions, and elective sign-ups that landed on the family this spring. The 9-year-old is still in elementary school and is the easier logistical case at the moment.

Her work life is what people inside large tech companies call "inside-the-machine." Most of her time is spent on org-internal surface area: skip-level 1:1s with the PM leads two levels down, calibration discussions ahead of perf, OKR drafting with her directs, coordination with sister product areas whose roadmaps touch hers, and the running back-channel with her SVP. She does very little external work — no press, limited customer time, occasional partner escalations. The product decisions she makes are filtered through other PMs; her direct artifacts are docs, review comments, staff agendas, and the occasional written narrative for an exec forum.

The current 35-day window, running from late April through late May 2026, lands her in the densest stretch of the half-year. Q2 OKRs are being graded in the first half of the window — her team is finalizing scores, writing the retro narratives, and surfacing the misses that need explanation upward. Q3 planning starts before Q2 grading finishes, which is normal. Layered on top is an exec review with her SVP about a strategic pivot in her area; the review is the kind that determines headcount allocation and whether a sister org absorbs part of her surface. She is preparing the narrative document for that review across several weeks, with input from her directs and at least one peer VP.

The personal calendar in the same window is shaped by the middle-school transition for the 12-year-old. There is a parent orientation, a placement step of some kind, and the usual end-of-year elementary events for the 9-year-old. Aisha and Marcus are coordinating which of them takes which event. Her tolerance for evening screens is low during exec-review prep weeks; she tends to be off her laptop by the time the kids are in bed and back on it after, in the kitchen, for an hour or so before sleeping.
