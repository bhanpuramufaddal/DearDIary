# Channels

A reference of how Aisha actually uses each communication and work surface in a normal week. Observational notes, not a profile of the tools themselves.

## Email — work Gmail (the @google.com account)

This is the channel where the formal work of her job lands. David Wexler sends her the agenda for the Tuesday 4 p.m. biweekly here on Monday afternoons, and the read-aheads for the SVP's portfolio reviews come through here as Google Docs attachments. Brendan Walsh, David's chief of staff, owns the threads that route retro narratives and OKR submissions; she copies Priya on every reply to Brendan as a matter of habit. Rohit Sharma and Liam O'Connor — the two peer VPs who read the May 28 narrative early — got the read-ahead from her by email, with the doc link in the body and a one-paragraph framing she wrote herself rather than letting Priya draft it.

She processes the inbox in batches: thirty minutes from 6:30 to 7:00 a.m. at the kitchen island while the East Coast is already going, a sweep between meetings around 11:30, and a final pass after the kids are down between 9:15 and 10:00. She archives aggressively. Threads she does not act on within a day get a label and disappear from the primary view. She drafts long replies in the Gmail web client and types short replies from her phone. She does not use Inbox Zero as a system; she uses "anything older than three days is either dead or someone else's problem now."

## Email — personal Gmail

The school correspondence lives here. The JLS incoming-parents list sends a weekly digest she reads in full on Sunday evenings; the Palo Verde teacher Ms. Halverson sends class-wide updates that go to both parents and that Marcus also reads. The PAMF patient portal sends appointment reminders here, as do the kids' pediatrician at the Sand Hill campus and Dr. Lila Hutchinson's office at the Palo Alto Center. Joanie's Café reservations, Bird Dog confirmations, the Mendocino rental's pre-trip checklist from the property manager, the Camp Tawonga forms for Naima — all of it. She replies to the school threads from her phone in the gaps between meetings. She does not forward work email here and does not log into work Gmail from her personal devices.

## Google Chat (internal Spaces and DMs)

This is where most of the day actually happens. Her directs ping her in DMs and she answers within nine minutes during business hours, ninety minutes after dinner. Wei Chen sends a single message at the start of his Wednesday 1:1 with the three bullets he wants to cover; she has come to expect it. James Brennan writes in longer paragraphs and occasionally voice-notes, which Aisha tolerates from him and from no one else. Hannah Beckwith and Roman Saito, both late on Q3 input, have not been chased in writing yet — Aisha is sitting on those messages and intends to send them Wednesday morning in two separate DMs. Priya is in DM with her several times an hour during exec-prep weeks; the cadence drops to maybe a dozen exchanges on a quiet day.

She is in the staff Space for her org, the Spaces for each of her four PM-lead surfaces, two cross-org councils' Spaces, and the SVP's staff Space. She reads more than she writes in the council Spaces. Jennifer Park, the design lead, lives in DM with her around artifact reviews. David Wexler messages her in DM when he wants something off-record from the Tuesday biweekly — she reads his messages for what is not said and replies same-hour without exception. During exec-prep weeks her own messages get shorter and use fewer emoji; David has noticed this twice and mentioned it once. She has not changed it.

## Phone calls and FaceTime audio

The Sunday 4 p.m. call to Janelle is the one fixture. It runs an average of thirty-eight minutes; Janelle does most of the talking. Aisha takes it on FaceTime audio from the armchair by the front window. Outside that, she takes calls from Marcus during the day when something is actually going on — the dog, the HVAC bid, the time Idris's coach called the house number and Marcus forwarded the line. She does not take work calls on her cell. David Wexler has never called her. Dr. Hutchinson's office called once in February about a lab result and left a voicemail; Aisha listened to it in the car and called back from her desk. She does not have a voicemail-greeting metaphor about being busy. The greeting is the default.

## Google Calendar

The shared family calendar with Marcus is where the household actually runs. Pickups, AYSO matches, the JLS parent coffees, Janelle's surgery week in September that Aisha already has blocked. Marcus puts the smog check on it. Naima's bedtime check-ins on Wednesdays and Saturdays are not on the calendar but the JLS orientation evenings are. On Sundays at 4, after the call with Janelle, Aisha and Marcus open the laptop and walk through the next two weeks.

Her work calendar is dense. Priya holds the source of truth — she is the one who declines meetings on Aisha's behalf, who built the Monday 9 a.m. staff and the 4 p.m. Tuesday biweekly with David as immovable, who refused to put a fifth rehearsal slot for the May 28 review on it. Aisha sends calendar invites for 1:1s with her directs and uses Google's "find a time" suggestions across timezones for the rare external meeting. Brendan Walsh's invites for the SVP forums come with read-ahead deadlines in the description; Aisha treats them as hard.

## Apple Notes — the household Notes doc with Marcus

There is one shared Notes document Aisha and Marcus have kept since 2019, called "House." Smog check dates, the HVAC bidder list Marcus has been getting since March, what the kids are allergic to for babysitters, the running list of things to do at the Greer Road house when they get a free Saturday. Aisha edits it from her phone at the kitchen island. Marcus edits it from the converted-garage office. They do not discuss what goes in it; they put things in and they get done.
