# Day archetypes

These are the recurring day-shapes in Aisha's life. They are written as the baseline pattern before any particular week's pressure lands; an exec-prep week or a sick kid bends each shape rather than replacing it.

## On-campus Monday — staff day

Aisha is up at 6:25 and at the kitchen island on Greer Road with her laptop by 6:30, because the East Coast has been at it for three hours and David Wexler has usually already sent two Slack messages she needs to read before staff. Marcus runs the espresso machine; she takes the first of her two daily coffees standing up while reading. Breakfast is Greek yogurt with frozen blueberries and a tablespoon of peanut butter, eaten at the counter while she scans Stratechery on her phone. Naima reads at the counter and eats cereal; Idris is on the couch with the TV on. Marcus packs the lunches and walks both kids the seven blocks to Palo Verde Elementary on Louis Road by 8:10. Aisha is in the 2022 Rivian by 8:15, takes Middlefield down to Charleston and across to Shoreline, and is at her parking spot at MP-9 in twelve minutes. The 8:30 with Priya is from her car when she's late and from the second-floor glass-walled office when she's on time. Staff runs at nine in the MP-9 conference room next to her office — eleven directs, sixty minutes, Priya in the corner with her laptop — and then the day collapses into back-to-back from ten until five-fifteen. Lunch is a salad from the MP-9 café on a tray brought back to her desk; she eats it while reading a Q3 OKR draft or on a 1:1 with the camera off.

She is in the Rivian at 5:25 and on Greer by 5:45 most Mondays, in time for dinner at 6:30. Marcus cooks; the rotation since his calcium-score result in October has been Mediterranean-leaning — salmon and lentils one night, a sheet-pan chicken and a salad another. The kids are in bed between 8:30 and 9, and Monday is one of Marcus's nights for the bedtime check-in with Naima. Aisha is back on the laptop at the kitchen island from 9:15 to about 10:30, working through the Eastern-time inbox and writing the agenda for Tuesday's 4 p.m. biweekly with David. The laptop closes by 10:28; she is in bed by 10:35.

## Heads-down Tuesday at the kitchen island

Tuesday she stays on Greer. She is up at 6:25, in the kitchen by 6:30, in the rotation of a cardigan over one of the five black T-shirts. Marcus is in the South San Francisco Stripe office on Tuesdays, so he is out the door in the 2019 Volvo by 7:10. Aisha takes the kids to Palo Verde herself on Tuesdays — the seven blocks down Greer with Tilda on the leash and Idris on his scooter and Naima walking ahead. She is back at the kitchen island by 8:20, second coffee in hand, Tilda asleep under the stool. The morning is three or four 1:1s with directs done from the dining-room table where she has the second monitor; Hannah Beckwith's 1:1 lands at 1:30 and is the one Aisha has been quietly working around, because Hannah is one of the two PM leads late on Q3 input and the conversation will not surface it unless Aisha does. Lunch is leftovers warmed in the microwave or a Sweetgreen order brought to the front porch.

The 4 p.m. biweekly with David is the meeting the rest of the week is organized around. Aisha takes it from the kitchen island with the camera on and Tilda visible at the bottom of the frame; David takes it from his home in Los Altos Hills. It runs forty-five minutes most weeks and sixty during exec-prep. By 4:50 she is in workout clothes for the 5K loop she has run for nine years now — out Greer to Greer Park, along the path behind Palo Verde, back via Loma Verde — and home by 5:35, showered by 5:50. Dinner at 6:30, kids in bed between 8:30 and 9; Aisha is back on the laptop from 9:15 to 10:30 cleaning up what the biweekly produced. Tuesday is the day she writes the longest Slack messages and the cleanest doc paragraphs, because Tuesday is the day with the fewest interruptions.

## Mid-week on campus — Wednesday

Wednesday is on campus and is the day the skip-levels happen. Aisha is up at 6:25, breakfast and coffee at the kitchen island, in the Rivian by 8:15, at MP-9 by 8:30. The 1:1 with Wei Chen is at 10 a.m.; it is the cleanest standing 1:1 on her week, thirty minutes, two written agenda items each, both of them done. The two skip-level 1:1s are in the early afternoon back-to-back with PM leads two layers down, and these are the meetings Priya makes her keep on the calendar even when exec-prep weeks make her want to cancel them. Lunch is at the MP-9 café standing at one of the high tables with Jennifer Park when their calendars line up. The 3-to-5 p.m. block is the one Aisha has identified in her own running self-assessment as the block in which she most often says something she wants to revise the next morning, and on Wednesdays she tries to keep it light. She is back in the Rivian at 5:25 and on Greer by 5:45.

Wednesday night is Naima's bedtime check-in. Aisha goes into the room around 8:30 with the JLS placement portal already refreshed once on her phone — she has been refreshing it twice a day for two weeks — and sits on the foot of the bed with whatever question Naima has. The structured version, where Naima has a printed elective form or a friend-list question to work through, goes well; the unstructured version, where Naima just wants Aisha there without an agenda, is the one Aisha has lately been catching herself reaching for structure inside of. She is out of the room by 9:05. The 9:15-to-10:30 block at the kitchen island is the writing block; on Wednesdays it is usually the consolidated Q3 OKR draft she owes David, and on the Wednesdays where the JLS portal still has not updated she also writes a draft email to the JLS office that she does not send.

## Friday at the kitchen island

Friday is the day she protects. She is up at 6:25, breakfast on Fridays is the egg-and-cheese on a toasted English muffin she has eaten on Fridays since 2018, and Marcus is the one who makes it. On the first Friday of every month she is in the Rivian by 7:50 and at Joanie's Café at 405 California Avenue by 8:00 for the standing breakfast with Dara Okafor, which they have not missed since November 2022; the breakfast is forty-five minutes, and she is back at the kitchen island by 9:15 with the second coffee. On the other Fridays of the month the morning starts at the kitchen island instead. Either way the 9:30-to-12:30 block is the cleanest writing block of her week — the one piece of long-form writing that has to be hers, the hiring review for the director-level PM Wei is shortlisting, the read-ahead memo for the next exec forum. The Thursday 2 p.m. with Priya has already framed what gets written.

The afternoon she takes a walk — out the back of Greer, around the block past Palo Verde and along the Loma Verde path, listening to Hard Fork in her AirPods, answering nothing on Slack. She is back by 3:30 in time to be at Palo Verde for the 3:05 pickup if Marcus is on the Caltrain home and asked her to take it. Idris has AYSO Region 26 practice from 4:30 to 6:00 some Fridays at the Mayfield Soccer Complex on El Camino Real; Marcus usually runs the soccer logistics with Coach Mark Petersen. Friday dinner is pizza from Terún on California Avenue when there is time to pick it up and from Pizza My Heart when there isn't. Twice a month Aisha and Marcus go out without the kids — Bird Dog on Ramona or Tamarine on University — with Sienna, the junior at Gunn High School, watching the kids and paid by 10:30. They are home by 10:15 every time.

## Saturday

Saturday morning starts later. Aisha is up at 7:15 and runs first — the same 5K loop out to Greer Park, along the path behind Palo Verde, back via Loma Verde — and is home by 8:10 in time to make coffee while Marcus does pancakes for the kids. Naima takes Tilda for the long walk around the block on her own around nine and comes back to read at the kitchen counter for an hour and a half. Idris has AYSO games most Saturdays through the spring season, either at the Mayfield Soccer Complex on El Camino Real or at the Greer Park fields two blocks from the house; the family rotates which parent takes the sideline chair and which does the grocery run to Piazza's Fine Foods at 3922 Middlefield Road. Aisha is the fast and decisive grocery shopper — in and out in twenty-two minutes with the week's list off the shared Notes document — and Marcus is the slow one, so she usually does Piazza's.

The afternoon is errands, kids' playdates, sometimes the Mitchell Park library, sometimes a stop at the CVS on Middlefield for the magnesium and the vitamin D. She does not open the laptop on Saturday afternoons during non-exec-prep weeks; during exec-prep weeks she opens it from 2 to 4 at the kitchen island and Marcus takes the kids to Greer Park without comment. Saturday is Naima's other bedtime check-in night, around 8:30 in the room with the door half-closed, and the conversation is usually about whatever Naima read that morning rather than about JLS. Saturday dinner is something Marcus grills on the redwood deck if the weather holds and something Aisha makes from the Piazza's haul if it doesn't. Aisha is in bed by 10:30 and reads twenty pages of the Min Jin Lee novel that is also on Naima's nightstand before she falls asleep.

## Sunday

Sunday is the household's planning day. Aisha is up at 7:30, makes the coffee for both adults, reads the New York Times morning briefing on her phone in the armchair by the front window while the kids sleep until eight-thirty. Breakfast is a long one — pancakes or French toast, Marcus cooking, the four of them at the table for forty-five minutes, which is the longest stretch of family time in a typical week. The morning is open. Sometimes the family drives over to the Stanford Dish for a walk; sometimes Aisha sits in the armchair by the front window with the Min Jin Lee novel and falls asleep within twenty minutes. The kids do whatever they do.

At 4 p.m. she calls Janelle in Atlanta on FaceTime from the bedroom on Greer with the door closed; the call averages thirty-eight minutes and Janelle does most of the talking — the week's news from Beecher Street, the substitute-teaching day at North Atlanta High, what Jamal's kids did at Sunday dinner. At 5 she sits at the kitchen island with Marcus and the shared Google Calendar open on the laptop, and they walk through the coming week — pickups, dinners, the meetings that need a babysitter, the doctor's appointments. Dinner is at 6:30, the family prestige show is on the living-room couch from 7:30 — currently the new season of Severance, which Naima is allowed to watch and Idris is not — and the kids are in bed by 9. Aisha is back on the laptop from 9:15 to 10:30 most Sundays, prepping Monday's staff agenda and the week's narrative additions, and the laptop closes at 10:28.

## Atlanta travel — outbound red-eye

Two or three times a year she flies back to Atlanta, and the pattern is consistent enough to be its own day-shape. On the morning of an outbound day she works a normal Friday from the kitchen island — she books the late-night Delta out of SFO so the workday is not lost — and at 6:30 she eats dinner with the family on Greer, does the bedtime check-in with Naima if the trip falls on a Wednesday or Saturday, and is in the Rivian at 8:15 for the drive up 101 to SFO. Marcus drops her at the Terminal 1 curb; she is through PreCheck by 9:00 and at the gate by 9:20. The flight is the Delta 757 that pushes back at 10:25 p.m. Pacific and lands at Hartsfield-Jackson at 5:55 a.m. Eastern. She does not work on the plane. She sleeps in a window seat with the noise-cancelling AirPods and the downloaded Beyoncé country album on a loop, and gets between two and three hours.

She is in a Lyft from the ATL ground-transportation curb by 6:30 a.m., on I-75/85 south, and at the back door of 1247 Beecher Street SW in Cascade Heights by 7:10. Janelle is up and at the kitchen table with the paper. The first hours back are slow — coffee in the kitchen she grew up in, the screened side porch she has not used since she was sixteen, the Buick LeSabre in the driveway some of the years and not others. She works from the dining-room table on her laptop for most of the trip; the East Coast is on her time zone for once and the calendar runs three hours earlier than usual. Jamal comes over for Sunday dinner from Decatur. The AME church on Cascade Road is the one place she goes on a Sunday morning when she is in town, sitting through it without resentment because Janelle expects it. The trip home is the Saturday-night red-eye back, landing at SFO at 6:50 a.m. Pacific, Marcus picking her up at arrivals with both kids still in their pajamas in the back of the Volvo.
